# Journal compatibility for operator reconciliation v0.2

**Accepted on November 21, 2026.** A foreground shipping worker can stop after replacing `shipping.py` but before it records the write. In that case, a restarted v0.1 reader returns the original dispatch and the workspace stays held. This contract gives a designated operator one way to record what happened without changing the run. The maintainer approved the configured host observer as the sole evidence source. The maintainer accepted the disk format, cutover and operator command after PR #57 passed post-merge verification. [Acceptance and verification](https://github.com/nnennandukwe/governed-agent-autonomy-patterns/issues/32#issuecomment-7071614794) [Maintainer decision](https://github.com/nnennandukwe/governed-agent-autonomy-patterns/issues/32#issuecomment-7071337244) · [Accepted recovery design](../adr/0009-durable-agent-run-reconciliation.md#who-can-resolve-an-unknown-effect)

The existing [v0.1 contract](journal-enrollment-v0.1.md) and both public GAAP contracts retain their versions. The enrolled host continues to use one private Linux ext4 workspace, one cooperating foreground writer, the original permanent lock and the independent volume health authority. The supervisor remains responsible for excluding uncooperative writers and for the custody of independent evidence. A process-death test leaves the kernel running. Hardware power-loss qualification and hostile same-user protection remain outside this profile.

## One interrupted attempt

Suppose the original history ends with intent and dispatch. Stopping the writer just before or after the target rename left exactly that prefix on Linux. The two attempts had different identities. One workspace still held the original target and the other held the replacement. With neither final observation, both duplicate calls returned the retained attempt and both fresh submissions stayed held. The released implementation produced these results on merged commit `209b87c`. [Process results and raw packet](https://github.com/nnennandukwe/governed-agent-autonomy-patterns/actions/runs/44964349724)

Under v0.2, an authorized operator can later present the configured observer's retained record of the identified rename and proof that the original writer has stopped. After admission, the reader returns the original unsealed dispatch plus a separate ruling that the write occurred. A duplicate couldn't produce a receipt or perform the write again. If another valid observation later contradicted that ruling, both observations would remain available, all principals would face a workspace hold and any completed intervening run would keep its original receipt. The operator decides an effect outcome. Only the original live engine could have sealed that attempt.

## Reading both formats

This contract keeps one slot, one journal and the permanent lock. The released v0.1 reader uses the anchor format before it interprets history. Its enrollment command and shipping writer also read that anchor **after** acquiring the lock. A v0.2 reader may read a confirmed v0.1 pair under the original historical profile. It can't add a ruling, change the format or start a v0.2 submission on that pair. A separately authorized conversion makes the slot available to v0.2 writers. Ordinary enrollment, reads, retries and submissions can't initiate conversion. [v0.1 contract](journal-enrollment-v0.1.md) · [Anchor validation and locking](../../adapters/journal/src/storage.rs) · [Shipping admission](../../adapters/journal/src/storage/writer.rs)

| On-disk anchor | Released v0.1 client | v0.2 client |
| --- | --- | --- |
| v0.1 active, confirmed | Existing v0.1 rules apply, including the workspace hold. | Read the original history. An eligible operator can request conversion against this exact pair. |
| v0.2 cutover | The version check yields `unsupported_storage`. | Report `cutover_pending` to an authorized reader, withhold keyed history and block dispatch. Only the matching authorized conversion can proceed. |
| v0.2 active, confirmed | The version check yields `unsupported_storage`. | Validate both profiles and the entire journal, including all rulings and any pending evidence hold, before returning a keyed answer or considering dispatch. |
| Missing, unsupported, inconsistent or unconfirmed pair | Existing refusals apply. | Hold the slot. Preserve the files. An older decoder, another slot or a fresh enrollment can't supply the answer. |

Current read permission, a busy lock, unavailable provisions or an unhealthy volume can stop either client before the format check. Every waiting writer must open and validate the current anchor and journal under the permanent lock. A reader can finish a v0.1 read under its existing shared lock before the conversion starts. Once the v0.2 fence is visible, an old client can't overlook a later dispute and return a clear history. There's no downgrade operation.

## A versioned journal with the original attempts

The closed v0.2 journal retains `workspace_id,store_id,profile_digest,generation,attempts`, sets `format` to `gaap.journal/0.2` and adds `reconciliation_profile_digest,rulings`. The original `profile_digest` still identifies the immutable v0.1 historical decoder, policy and evidence roles. The second digest pins the separately provisioned host-observer policy. Compare both with the supervisor's trusted configuration on every open.

The closed v0.2 anchor retains the v0.1 members, sets `format` to `gaap.journal-anchor/0.2` and adds `reconciliation_profile_digest,origin,pending`. Its phases are `cutover` and `active`. The immutable `origin` has exactly `source_generation,source_attempt_count,source_anchor_digest,source_journal_digest,initial_journal_digest`. The counts describe the confirmed v0.1 source. The three digests hash the complete canonical source anchor, complete canonical source journal and exact initial v0.2 journal, respectively. Compare complete canonical bytes as well as hashes when both objects are available. These hashes never replace the frozen receipt-body and result-body seals.


The reconciliation profile is a closed, host-provisioned canonical object with `format,workspace_id,store_id,profile_digest,effect_observer`. Its format is `gaap.shipping-reconciliation/0.2`. The two IDs, historical profile digest and observer must match the original enrollment. Hash its complete canonical bytes to obtain `reconciliation_profile_digest`. This version fixes the verification rules and gives only this observer a witness role. The operator's rights come from current supervisor authorization. The host pins an independently authenticated, append-only index for this profile before conversion. The index must authenticate an original observer deposit. For this profile, cap each uncompressed candidate and its referenced source bytes together at 2 MiB, and the separate candidate archive at 32 MiB and 48 records. These ceilings match the existing shipping archive’s resource envelope. Keep both archives separately pinned and enforce their bounds before allocation. An oversized or unavailable deposit can’t authorize a ruling.

At conversion, copy the canonical `attempts` array byte for byte, including admission order, keys, original requests, every fact and sealed envelope. Set `rulings` to `[]`. Retain the source generation G. A format change alone records no new evidence. Compare the format, G and whole-file digest to identify each version. An unexpected digest at the same version and generation still holds the slot. On later reads, the original prefix of `source_attempt_count` attempts must reproduce the v0.1 source bytes and initial v0.2 bytes under their respective headers and digests. Reconstruct the source active anchor from its unmodified v0.1 fields, the pinned identities, G and the source journal digest, and verify its complete hash too. A later process can't resume a source attempt.

Each subsequent original fact or ruling advances the generation once. Preserve every prior entry. Each ruling records its publication generation in `recorded_generation` and its `original_fact_count`, the total number of original facts already present at that publication. Original facts retain admission order, and only the current live attempt can grow before its writer exits. Require each frontier to include at least the full converted prefix and to end at the last retained fact of a whole attempt. The named attempt and its original dispatch must already exist at that frontier. An interrupted attempt can end at dispatch, but it can never gain more live facts after the operator acquired the lock. Reject a ruling inside an attempt or before its own dispatch. Insert rulings at those frontiers in generation order to check each subsequent admission and dispatch against the hold that applied then. Validate the source prefix with the v0.1 reader. Starting at G, require every subsequent generation and frontier to match that chronology. This avoids inventing an original generation for an old fact. A later dispute holds future dispatch across the workspace and preserves the earlier authorized effect and receipt.

## Converting a confirmed slot

For a **new** workspace, use the accepted provisioning and enrollment protocol once, withhold shipping grants, then authorize conversion against its confirmed generation-zero pair. Existing workspaces use the same conversion at their actual confirmed generation. The administrative command `gaap-journal convert` takes no caller arguments or stdin. The supervisor supplies current conversion authority for the named slot, the expected source generation, both source digests and the approved new policy through its trusted handoff. A v0.1 enrollment privilege or a submission key alone can't authorize this operation. The v0.1 contract has no migration path. The maintainer authorized implementation and qualification. Converting a deployed slot requires separate authorization against its confirmed source pair.

1. Acquire the existing exclusive health lock, then the existing exclusive slot lock. Hold both through confirmation. Check health `0`, ordered ext4, mount and device, the pinned workspace, control and store directories, lock and independent health identity. Recheck owner, modes, links, directory entries and both canonical files. Require one confirmed v0.1 active pair, the original archive and all its history, including partial attempts, sealed refusals and sealed unknown outcomes. Establish that no previous worker can commit. A validated unresolved dispatch **can** be converted and retains its hold. A v0.1 reservation, a one-ahead snapshot, foreign scratch or unproven writer quiescence can't be converted. [Storage implementation](../../adapters/journal/src/storage/linux.rs) · [Foreground executor proof](../plans/issue-32-journal-executor.md)
2. Precompute the exact successor and the three anchor/journal digests. Verify the full candidate and the canonical original attempts. Check all bounds before touching the slot. Replace `anchor.json` with the v0.2 `cutover` anchor. It records `origin`, G and the **source** journal digest. Sync the control directory and reread the fence before touching `journal.json`. The cutover anchor sets `pending` to null. The first active anchor must do the same.
3. Replace `journal.json` with the initial v0.2 bytes. Sync the journal directory and reread the anchored candidate. Replace `anchor.json` with the v0.2 `active` anchor at G and the **destination** journal digest. Sync the control directory, reread and validate both files, then acknowledge. Each replacement uses exclusive staging in the destination directory, full write, flush, file sync, checked writer close, identity recheck, atomic rename and directory sync. Apply the existing volume health guards at every mutating syscall. [Publication sequence](../../adapters/journal/src/storage/publication.rs)

| What a fresh process verifies after interruption | Permitted action under the same fence |
| --- | --- |
| Exact healthy v0.1 pair, before a durable v0.2 fence | Reauthorize against that exact source. A v0.1 writer may have advanced it before conversion took the lock. A changed source requires a new decision against the current pair. |
| Exact v0.2 `cutover` anchor and original source journal | Recheck authorization, both policies, immutable origin and quiescence. Confirm the visible fence by syncing its file and directory. Rebuild and compare the exact candidate, then continue. |
| Exact v0.2 `cutover` anchor and intended destination journal | Validate the complete destination and reconstruct the exact source from its preserved attempts. Reconfirm file and directory sync for the fence and journal before activating. |
| Exact confirmed v0.2 active lineage, including a lost conversion reply | An authorized repeat can reconfirm the visible pair by sync and reread and report its current generation. Compare the immutable origin with the requested conversion. Preserve any newer history, pending candidate or dispute. |
| Health `P` or `1`, failed sync or checked close, missing authority, unknown scratch ownership, digest or identity mismatch, unsupported state or stale origin | Withhold mutation and keyed answers as applicable. Preserve surviving bytes and stages. A restart can't clear quarantine, adopt an abandoned stage by matching its contents, roll back the journal or treat a lost response as proof of non-conversion. |

The source and destination keep the v0.1 limits of 16 KiB per anchor, 8 MiB per complete journal, 12 retained attempts, 256 KiB each for raw and canonical proposals, nesting depth 64 and nonnegative JCS-safe counters. Count the new header and every ruling inside the journal bound. Stage and validate the largest required control record within the anchor bound. A valid source that can't fit the successor stays on v0.1 and retains its history and holds. An exact-fit store can remain readable after conversion. Ruling intake needs room for its own generation, encoded entry and archive evidence. A fresh shipping attempt also needs the five remaining counters already required by the writer. [Admission bounds](../../adapters/journal/src/storage/writer.rs)

The new anchor's `pending` field protects an intake independently of space for another journal generation. Every v0.2 client must inspect it under the lock, even when the active anchor and journal otherwise match. A current, authorized reader can return the last confirmed history and the hold. Fresh dispatch requires a confirmed pair and a clear intake. The operator procedure below defines the only transition that can clear a pending intake.

## Who may admit an observation

`gaap-journal reconcile ATTEMPT_ID EVIDENCE_DIGEST` takes exactly two positional selectors. The attempt ID is 32 lowercase hexadecimal digits. The digest is `sha256:` followed by 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits. The command accepts no report body, path, URL, verdict or actor override. The designated operator can point to bytes the host already holds. The operator can't choose what those bytes prove.

The supervisor supplies the authenticated principal and enrolled slot through its private handoff. Check current read and reconciliation-write rights for that workspace before revealing whether either selector exists. Enrollment, submission, ordinary read access and possession of an attempt ID grant no reconciliation rights. Acquire the permanent slot lock exclusively. Revalidate the supervisor's bindings, current rights, volume health, confirmed anchor, entire original history, previous rulings and intake guard under that lock. Establish that the original foreground writer can no longer commit, including through a surviving child or a retained permit. A caller-named PID, elapsed lease or momentarily idle lock can't establish that property. The operator supplies admission authority. Only the enrolled host observer can supply effect evidence. [Owner's witness decision](https://github.com/nnennandukwe/governed-agent-autonomy-patterns/issues/32#issuecomment-7071337244)

### Independent source and evidence checks

Resolve the digest through the separately pinned, read-only host evidence index for this workspace. It maps an immutable canonical source record to its digest and the authenticated observer that deposited it. Pin the store, historical `profile_digest`, `reconciliation_profile_digest`, observer identity and private archive custody independently of that record. Multiple records from the same role may concern one attempt. Retain each original source and all bytes it references, within the profile's bounds. Check canonical encoding, limits, original bytes and their hashes at admission and on subsequent reads. The archive and transport must identify who produced the bytes. A matching digest or an `actor_id` in those bytes can't authenticate the producer. A human report, executor return, verifier PASS, grant source, inspector output or file found on disk can't substitute for the configured observer. Slice 2's single effect capsule per binding stays intact. [Original archive](../../adapters/journal/src/live/archive.rs) · [Original witness](../../adapters/journal/src/live/witness.rs)

Recompute the v0.1 attempt binding from the two enrolled IDs, historical profile, original principal, key, attempt ID, proposal digest and both request digests. Verify the exact retained proposal, effect target and proposed artifact digest, original run and effect IDs, historical observer role and dispatch's before subject and FileObservation. The source must identify this invocation, its pinned workspace and target, its observation interval, and the observer's original input, event and terminal records. Reject a source bound to another invocation even if two requests named the same file and proposed identical content.

For the shipping executor, verify the observer's source records under the one-writer profile. Check its independently observed pre-effect target identity and content against dispatch, its opened stage and event prefix against the single staged inode, and any output observation against the original target and proposed UTF-8 bytes. Recompute artifact and repository subject digests from retained original bytes and identity records or authenticated, resolved source records that supply those inputs. The repository subject covers `TASK.md`, `shipping.py` and `deployment.json`. Reconstruct event order and coverage from the original records. Reject queue overflow, a missing watch, an unobserved interval, ambiguous moves, a possible second writer or a lost stage pin wherever they defeat the claimed inference. A normalized index entry, a later filesystem snapshot, a claimed disposition or a `covers_through_stop` boolean can't fill a missing observation. Existing archives that lack the inputs for a new ruling remain usable under their original read contract. [Shipping observation](../../adapters/journal/src/live/observation.rs) · [Storage v0.1](journal-enrollment-v0.1.md)

| Disposition | Evidence the operator must verify |
| --- | --- |
| `replaced` | The independent observer associated the one-use invocation with the pinned stage. A stage move-from and target move-to on the pinned directory have the same nonzero cookie. The resulting target has the stage's identity, the proposed artifact digest and the independently observed output subject. The qualified writer model excludes another possible committer. Keep this observed change even if verification or publication failed. |
| `not_started` | An authoritative observation covers the earliest possible effect entry through definitive stop. It shows this invocation never entered the effect and no callable path or outstanding permit could start it afterward. A watcher first armed at stage creation or a dead worker with an empty event log can't supply this proof. |
| `stopped_before_rename` | The observer identifies this invocation's entry, its specific pre-rename failure and the end of every possible commit path. For a bound shipping stage, verify complete directory coverage, its deletion, the held descriptor's zero link count and the original target and subject at stop. Establish the one-use path can't obtain a second stage or retry after that stop. Worker termination or lock release alone can't tell whether it renamed before dying. |
| Insufficient evidence | Report the missing or ambiguous element. Retain the source under host custody. Preserve the previous disposition and hold. A missing observation can't overrule an established one. |

The supervisor can authenticate the original launch, exclusion and termination of the specific writer. It can't decide the filesystem outcome for the observer. The deployed witness can lose its channel after binding a stage. In the slice 2 witness-loss case, independently checking the target showed a write even though the surviving grant, before observation and stage identity left the journal held. That surviving prefix alone can't support a ruling. [Verified process evidence](https://github.com/nnennandukwe/governed-agent-autonomy-patterns/issues/32#issuecomment-7071309464)

## Intake, ruling and dispute

Only a dispatched original invocation can receive a ruling. Check admissible evidence against the original known live observation and all prior admitted sources for this binding, including after publication or later independent submissions. A known outcome alongside an old `unresolved` observation can settle the open effect. Two fully validated sources that can't both describe this one invocation create a dispute. Examples include replacement against zero commits, never entering against a proven pre-rename failure after entry, or different final stage identities or output subjects for a single rename. Differences in collection time or source digest alone don't establish a conflict. Keep all prior facts and sources, record the competing ruling and hold the workspace permanently under this profile. There's no command to choose a winner or clear a dispute.

**Durable intake guard.** Store `pending` in the active anchor. Its closed value is null or an object with `attempt_id,evidence_digest,intake_by`. The supervisor supplies the authenticated principal who requested intake. For a new pair, establish that the pinned index contains its immutable record before reading its contents. Write the selector and principal into the anchor under the permanent lock. Retain the same journal generation and digest. Use the existing file and directory sync, checked close, health and reread protocol. Only after this anchor is confirmed may the operator read the candidate and make a ruling. Recheck current rights immediately before the ruling append or definitive rejection under the supervisor’s authority fence. Revocation before that decision leaves the intake pending. Reserve encoded room in the 16 KiB anchor at conversion for the largest such value. Disk space for staging can still run out. If the update provably failed before publication, report no intake, consume no evidence and preserve the old confirmed state. An uncertain write or sync invokes the independent storage hold. A waiting client must reopen the anchor under the lock. A pending selector blocks fresh dispatch even after a healthy restart.

Retain the same pending object across the ruling's journal rename and its separate anchor confirmation. Any currently authorized reconciliation operator for this workspace may retry the exact pending pair. If no ruling has committed, the operator must independently revalidate the evidence and record their own principal as `admitted_by`. The original `intake_by` identifies who requested intake. It grants no authority to a retry. After a lost reply, return the stored admitting principal and original ruling even if another authorized operator later requests readback. Reconfirm a visible matching anchor and journal by sync and reread before acknowledging a durable result or clearing the pointer. An ordinary journal one generation ahead of its anchor remains `unconfirmed`. This contract gives an operator no authority to promote a one-ahead ruling. It stays held and retains the source and both files for diagnosis.

During a pending intake, only an authorized retry of that exact pair may settle it. Keep the guard if source access, grant checking, quiescence, interpretation or publication is uncertain. An authenticated retry may clear it after a confirmed ruling is visible and independently reconstructible, including a ruling that leaves a dispute. It may also clear it after a definitive rejection from intact immutable inputs, such as a proved binding mismatch or a complete but insufficient source. Missing or damaged previously pinned inputs aren't definitive rejections. Clearing the guard after definitive rejection restores the independently derived hold from the existing history. It can never remove a dispute. A new candidate, freed disk space or a healthy volume can't discharge an earlier intake.

A full journal, exhausted generation count or failed writeback can't discard an admitted contradiction. Preserve the whole immutable source, the existing ruling and the pending pointer across process death and restart. Report `capacity` or the appropriate storage hold, and keep all new dispatch disabled while the contradiction can't be recorded. Never replace older evidence to make room. Competing intakes serialize. If the guard is already pending, leave the second source in its private archive and return `busy` until the first intake is settled. An unreadable anchor or missing previously pinned source keeps the slot held. Starting a new slot for the same workspace can't bypass that withdrawal.

**Ordering and repeat requests.** The closed ruling object has exactly `attempt_id,binding,evidence_digest,observer_id,admitted_by,disposition,reason,after,recorded_generation,original_fact_count`. Use the v0.1 attempt ID, canonical binding, digest and principal types. The reason is one of `observed_replacement`, `never_entered_before_stop` or `failed_before_rename`, matching the three admissible dispositions in the evidence table. The counters are nonnegative JCS-safe integers. Use the original FileObservation for `after` on a replacement and null on either negative. Derive conflicts from the validated facts and retained rulings. A later writer can't clear a dispute by changing a status flag. Retain every original fact in place. Merge successive rulings into the original fact order at their recorded frontiers. Evaluate each new dispatch against the holds in effect at that point. A ruling can permit a later fresh attempt. A subsequent contradiction holds future dispatch from its own admission onward. Preserve that intervening attempt and any sealed receipt it produced. A later successful attempt can't resolve the dispute.

An identical source for the same attempt returns the original ruling with its original admitting principal and recorded generation, plus the current workspace hold and generation. The retry adds no row, usage or generation. A different admissible corroborating source can create its own record under the same bounds. A reply may acknowledge new evidence only after the journal and confirming anchor are durably committed. When the durable result is uncertain, the operator retries the same pair. The operator command never invokes an executor, grants approval, recreates an observation, seals a run or changes a receipt. [Reconciliation in ADR 0009](../adr/0009-durable-agent-run-reconciliation.md)

### Command results and disclosure

| Condition | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Malformed selectors or extra arguments | Usage error before reading a slot. |
| Missing current workspace read or reconciliation-write right | `forbidden` before selector lookup. |
| Unknown attempt or source in this authorized workspace | No ruling. Return `not_found` without distinguishing an object in another workspace. |
| Intact attempt never dispatched, wrong binding, ineligible source role or objectively insufficient record | No ruling. Return `ineligible_evidence` with a bounded reason. Keep existing facts and their hold. A missing dependency of an already-admitted ruling instead fails intact-store validation. |
| Valid new ruling or exact durable repeat | `recorded` with the original stored ruling and the current generation, derived effect status and workspace hold. A conflict returns the competing ruling and `disputed` effect status. A repeat of an earlier ruling after a dispute still reports `disputed`. |
| Another live writer or other pending intake, unsupported format, invalid history, lost source or uncertain storage | Use the applicable `busy`, `unsupported_storage`, `invalid_store`, `unconfirmed` or `unavailable` result. Report `capacity` only for an intact store that can't fit the new record. Withhold keyed facts whenever integrity can't be established. |

An authorized historical reader still selects its own `(principal,key)`. Preserve `partial` or `published` and return the original attempt, result and receipt exactly as validated. Return any operator rulings, their derived effect status and an applicable pending or disputed hold separately. An authorized absent key or conflicting proposal may learn the workspace hold under the existing response contract. It can't reveal another principal's keys, attempts, sources, receipts or ruling details. A matched reader sees only its attempt's ruling projections and evidence digests. Private raw reports, operator handoff records and other attempts' details stay with the host. The designated operator's command can return the selected ruling and current workspace-level hold within its scoped rights. It doesn't enumerate records or disclose another caller's proposal.

Duplicate submission remains a historical lookup with current read authorization. A changed canonical proposal conflicts. A new key and new host-issued identities require every preceding effect to be settled, the intake guard clear, writer quiescence, a fresh subject and current approval, policy, capability and budget. Reconciliation grants none of those rights. An unsealed attempt stays unsealed and a sealed refusal or failure keeps its original outcome, even when an operator later proves a write.

## Compatibility evidence and qualification cases

The released v0.1 commands ran in a separate Ubuntu arm64 guest on ordered ext4. Production binaries came from `615dd36`. The harness came from its merged tree at `209b87c`. In disposable slots, `lookup`, `enroll` and fresh `journal-submit` returned `unsupported_storage` for either new format tag. All three returned `invalid_store` for a v0.1 journal with an added `rulings` member or an unknown fact on another key's sealed attempt. An old writer already running when the fixture replaced the anchor waited for the permanent slot lock and then refused the new anchor. Independent snapshots showed the same slot files, health, archived evidence and target before and after each call. The five-case evidence packet has SHA-256 `c7ea55eb5bc3e7d9d8b5384143fcfab1af47d89971bd59462161b117f4d02f89`. The fixture supplied the format markers. The 19 passing ADR model tests cover logical recovery, including conflicting sources and immutable seals.
